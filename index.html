Video & Production

Film and video shoot location in Roanoke

Timber posts hold up a high, vaulted and beamed ceiling. The plank floor is the original, worn the way a century of work wears wood, and the brick has never been covered. Ductwork runs where it has always run.

The light is the reason people book it. Windows down three walls and a run of skylights over the middle, so it moves all day — hard and raking in the morning, long window-pane shadows the length of the floor by late afternoon, and the lit Mill Mountain Star in the south glass after dark.

Load-in is a 4,500 lb freight elevator with a lot right outside the door.

See the floor plan with dimensions — 59′4″ by 58′, nine posts on a roughly fifteen-foot grid, so you can block the shot before you get here.

The building

Built in 1912 for the Virginia Can Company.

Cans came off these floors for forty years: Virginia Can, then United States Can from 1927, then Continental Can, which kept the lines running until 1951. Kenrose Manufacturing turned the floors into a clothing factory in 1952. S. H. Heironimus, Roanoke’s own department store, bought the complex in 1963 and warehoused here into the early 2000s. Their painted sign is still on the wall, and the Continental Can ghost sign is still on the south parapet.

The building has been individually listed on the National Register of Historic Places since 22 February 2006, and is on the Virginia Landmarks Register. The ground floor is tenanted. The second floor is where the Star View Room is.

After dark the Mill Mountain Star sits framed in the south windows. That is where the room gets its name.
